For two years, every serious agent project I delivered had the same hidden cost: orchestration. Which model handles research? Which one summarizes? How do you run the slow parts in parallel and merge the results without losing the thread? At Microsoft Ignite 2026, Microsoft moved that problem into the product. Auto in Copilot Cowork now uses subagents: for complex tasks, it splits the work across several models that run side by side, each one using the model best suited to its part, and then brings everything back together into one finished result.

My take: this is a bigger deal than the model announcements that came with it. New models arrive every few weeks. A built-in way to divide, parallelize and recombine work is what turns a chat assistant into something you can delegate real work to.

What's new in Copilot Cowork at Ignite 2026 (image: Microsoft)

1. What really changes: one task, many models, one result

Until now, when you asked Cowork for something complex, you got one agent working through the steps. With Auto and subagents, Cowork breaks the task into parts and runs them in parallel. Microsoft's own example is a competitive analysis that can research every competitor in parallel and come back as a single brief.

The official screenshot makes this concrete. A user asks Cowork to research top marketing trends and create an Excel workbook comparing campaigns, target audiences, messaging, channels and engagement. Under "Background tasks ran for 2m 18s" you can see four subagents:

  • Research recent campaigns, on Claude Opus 5.5 (2m 18s)
  • Gather team feedback from Outlook and Teams, on GPT-6.1 Sol (1m 24s)
  • Find Q3 campaign results in SharePoint, on GPT-6.1 Sol (58s)
  • Look up Q2 channel budget, on Claude Sonnet 5.5 (22s)

Four subagents, three models, one workbook. The total time equals the longest branch, not the sum of all of them.

Cowork with Auto turned on, running subagents side by side on a task (image: Microsoft)

2. The underrated detail: the right model for each part

Look at the model badges again. The heavy research went to Claude Opus 5.5. The quick budget lookup went to Claude Sonnet 5.5. The Microsoft 365 work (Outlook, Teams, SharePoint) went to GPT-6.1 Sol. That's the routing decision I usually make by hand when I design multi-agent solutions in Microsoft Foundry or Copilot Studio, and here Auto makes it per subagent.

It also connects to the other Auto enhancement announced at Ignite: Auto can now auto-adjust reasoning effort for each task, using Microsoft's latest decision model, Microsoft-Decision-1.1, on every step. Quick lookups stay fast and complex work gets deeper reasoning. Put the two together and you get a system that decides both which model works on each part and how hard it thinks.

Cowork composer with Auto adjust effort on (image: Microsoft)

3. Risk and governance: more agents, same permissions

More moving parts means more questions from security teams. The good news is that the boundary doesn't move: Microsoft states that Cowork runs inside the Microsoft 365 security, compliance and governance organizations already use, and works within each user's existing permissions. In the example, the subagent that searches SharePoint only sees what the user can see.

What I'd still watch: data residency and retention per model. Some models in the Cowork model selector have their own requirements (Learn documents, for example, that Claude Fable models require data retention). If Auto can route parts of a task to different models, admins need to know which models are enabled in their tenant.

4. What admins should do now

  • Check whether your organization is in the Frontier program: Auto with effort levels and subagents is available today in Frontier.
  • Review which models are turned on for Cowork in your tenant, and their data retention requirements, in "Choose a model for Cowork" on Microsoft Learn.
  • Pick two or three multi-source tasks (research + internal data + a deliverable) to pilot, and compare time and quality against today's process.
  • Read the new Learn guide "Plan and implement business processes with Copilot Cowork" before you scale.

5. What makers and developers should do

Stop designing orchestration first. For tasks that live inside Microsoft 365, describe the outcome and let Auto split the work. Spend your effort on the parts Auto can't guess: where the authoritative data lives, what "done" looks like, and which steps need human approval.

Keep building custom multi-agent solutions where you need full control of the flow, specific tools or your own evaluation loop. But for the "research, gather, combine into a document" class of work, Cowork just raised the bar for what you get without writing orchestration code.
